The Introductory EA Fellowship

A six-week course on the fundamentals of effective altruism, run by us here in Amsterdam. Each week you spend about two hours on readings, videos and sometimes an exercise, then meet in person for a 90-minute discussion with your group and a facilitator.

Anyone can join. You don't need to be a student, you don't need any background in philosophy or economics, and you don't need to already agree with any of it. Curiosity and a willingness to think it through together are enough.

What it teaches is method: how to make your reasoning visible, how to compare ways of helping, how to act sensibly when the evidence is thin, and how to work out which problems deserve your effort. By the end you'll have a plan for increasing your impact through your career, your giving, or the way you spend your time.

What you'll cover

Each week has a theme, a set of readings and a discussion. The readings set out the arguments, and the sessions are where you test them: what holds up, what doesn't, and what it would mean for you.

The exact curriculum can differ between cohorts, but these are the kinds of topics you'll explore. The last week is for short final presentations, followed by an event where you meet the wider EA Amsterdam community.

What effective altruism is
The core ideas, and why some ways of helping achieve far more than others.
Global health and development
Comparing interventions on cost, evidence and scale.
Compassion and rationality
Whose interests count, from people far away to animals, and how to reason clearly when the stakes are high.
The long-term future
Why future generations might matter, and the risks that could shape what comes next.
Putting it into practice
Careers, giving and other ways to act, and drafting your own plan for impact.

How signing up works

  1. Let us know you're interested

    Fill in the short sign-up form before 20 December. It just tells us you'd like to join; it doesn't commit you to a time yet.

  2. Share your availability

    After 20 December we'll email you a follow-up form asking which times you're free.

  3. Get placed in a cohort

    We match you with a facilitator and other participants whose times fit yours, and your group starts in the week of 4 January.
