The Introductory EA Fellowship

Six weeks, one session a week, in a small group with a facilitator. Each week has a set of core materials and an exercise to work through beforehand — about an hour and a quarter of reading and half an hour on the exercise — and then you spend the session discussing it with the same handful of people.

Fellows decide together at the start of each session what is worth digging into, so it follows what your group actually found interesting rather than a script. Beyond the core materials there is recommended and further reading each week, all optional — useful if you already know some of this.

It is the single best way in. You get the ideas in a sensible order, the strongest objections alongside them, and people to argue with rather than a reading list you will never finish.

The curriculum

What the six weeks cover

The 2025 curriculum. It gets revised each round, so the emphasis shifts, but this is the shape of it.

  1. Week 1: Introduction to Effective Altruism

    Where the whole thing starts: how to compare problems at all, and why the differences between them are so much larger than people expect.

    Cause prioritisation, scale, solvability, neglectedness, impartiality, cause neutrality, the giving multiplier.

  2. Week 2: Global Health and Development

    The best-evidenced giving opportunities in the world, how they get evaluated, and what your money does at the margin.

    Cost-effectiveness, expected value, scope insensitivity, donations.

  3. Week 3: Compassion and Rationality

    Whose interests count, how moral circles have widened historically, and the thinking tools that keep you honest.

    Moral circle, animal welfare, moral progress, speciesism, radical empathy, biases, fallacies, confidence intervals, judgement calibration.

  4. Week 4: Longtermism

    Whether future people matter morally, and what follows if they do. The week that divides opinion most.

    Longtermism, epistemic uncertainty, consequentialism, person-affecting views, risk aversion.

  5. Week 5: Existential Risk

    Low-probability, extremely high-stakes risks — and a group exercise where you present one to each other.

    Existential risk, AI safety, pandemic prevention, nuclear security, run-away climate change.

  6. Week 6: Taking Action

    The practical week. You pick concrete first steps — a career move, a pledge, joining something — take them, and report back to the group.

    Careers, community, donations, and next steps.

Organisation spotlights

Alongside the readings, each week introduces an organisation actually doing the work — the Centre for Effective Altruism, GiveWell and Animal Charity Evaluators among them. Useful when you get to the point of wondering where to give or apply.

Exercises, not just reading

Every week has an exercise: setting up how your group wants to discuss, working out whether future people matter to you, preparing a short presentation on an existential risk. They are where the ideas turn into something you can use.

How the fellowship works

What we ask of fellows

Three habits the fellowship is built around. They are worth reading before you apply, because they are what makes the six weeks work.

Take ideas seriously

It is easy to enjoy batting an interesting thought around and then carry on exactly as before. Instead: how could I tell if this were true, and if it is, what should I be doing differently?

Disagreements are interesting

When thoughtful people with the same information reach opposite conclusions, that is worth being curious about rather than shrugging at. Often the answer matters.

Strong opinions, weakly held

Venture a guess, and say it clearly enough that someone can prove you wrong. Becoming a stronger thinker matters more than being right this week.

We do ask fellows to do the core reading before each session — it is what makes the discussion worth having, for you and for everyone else in your group.

After the fellowship

Where people usually go next.

A longer follow-on for people who finished the intro and want to go deeper into a cause area or into the harder arguments.

Running a group of your own is the fastest way to actually learn this material. We are usually looking for new facilitators and organisers.

One-to-one conversations, 80,000 Hours advising, and workshops on turning a vague direction into applications.

Projects and research

Theses, side projects and volunteering. If you have an idea, we can usually find you someone who has done something similar.

Questions people ask

Do I need to know anything about EA beforehand?

No. The fellowship is built for people starting from zero.

Do I have to agree with effective altruism?

No, and it is a better group when not everyone does. Week four in particular divides people. Plenty finish unconvinced and stay involved.

How much work is it really?

One to two hours of preparation a week, plus the session. If that is not realistic for the next six weeks, take a later cohort — arriving without having read is worse than not arriving.

What if I miss a week?

It happens — tell your facilitator. Missing more than a couple makes it hard for the group, so if you know it will be a bad six weeks, take the next cohort instead.

Is it only for students?

No. We run groups for students and for working people, usually at different times of day.

When does the next one start?

Each semester. Dates go on the calendar above and in our WhatsApp community as soon as they are set.
