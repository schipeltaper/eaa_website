The Introductory EA Fellowship

A practical course in doing good more effectively, run by Effective Altruism Netherlands. One session a week with a facilitator, with reading and an exercise to work through beforehand.

It teaches skills rather than conclusions: how to make your reasoning visible, how to compare interventions, how to act sensibly when the evidence is thin, and how to work out which problems deserve your effort. By the end you will have a plan for increasing your impact — through your career, your giving, your political voice, and your role in helping others do the same.

The people matter as much as the material. Past participants have gone into government, started organisations, done research at top universities, led teams at high-impact nonprofits, and moved serious money to effective causes. Some changed direction entirely; others found more impact in the path they were already on.

What you will learn

What the six weeks cover

This is a practical course rather than a survey of ideas. Five weeks each target one skill, and the later ones depend on the earlier — you cannot evaluate a charity or prioritise between problems without showing your working first. The sixth is a social.

  1. Week 1 — Reasoning transparency and the foundations of effective altruism

    Making your thinking visible, so others can engage with it, challenge it and help you improve it.

  2. Week 2 — Measuring and comparing impact

    QALYs, DALYs and WELLBYs as ways to compare interventions — and how to read a charity recommendation critically.

  3. Week 3 — Reasoning under uncertainty

    Expected value, calibration and forecasting. Not everything can be measured, and thin evidence is not a reason to stop thinking.

  4. Week 4 — Prioritising across causes

    The ITN framework — importance, tractability, neglectedness — for working out where your effort could matter most, and where the framework runs out.

  5. Week 5 — Your plan for impact

    Concrete intentions for your career, your giving, your political voice and your role in helping others do the same.

  6. Week 6 — Social

    No reading, no exercise, nothing to prepare. Six weeks in you will know these people properly; this is the evening for staying in touch with them and with the wider community.

Along the way

The cause areas we look at

The point is not to learn a list of approved causes. It is to end up able to evaluate them yourself and decide where you can contribute most.

Global health and development

Improving lives in the global south through proven interventions.

Animal welfare

Reducing the suffering of animals, particularly in factory farming.

Global catastrophic risks

Pandemics, nuclear war, AI disasters — whether you weigh them for people alive today or for the future.

Longtermism

Positively influencing the long-term future: not only avoiding catastrophe, but making good futures better.

How it runs

What a week looks like

The discussion is not a seminar where you talk about ideas. It is a workshop where you share what you have produced and help each other improve it, so come with your exercises done.

In the final week you bring it together in a presentation: the problem you have chosen, your career plan or advising application, your giving intention, and your plans for advocacy and for multiplying. Your group gives you feedback to sharpen it before you act on it.

Each week

Read
Background material for the week — 45–90 minutes
Quiz
A few multiple-choice questions to check it landed — 5–10 minutes
Practise
Exercises that build the week's skill — 30–60 minutes
Discuss
The group session, where you share your work — 75 minutes

Why it is built this way. The course is designed around self-determination theory: people stay motivated when they feel a sense of autonomy, competence and relatedness. So it works from your own values rather than telling you what to conclude, teaches skills with feedback as you go rather than one exam at the end, and puts you in a cohort rather than leaving you to read alone.

Questions people ask

Do I need to know anything about EA beforehand?

No. The fellowship is built for people starting from zero.

Do I have to agree with effective altruism?

No, and it is a better group when not everyone does. The course teaches you to evaluate these claims yourself, not to accept them. Plenty of people finish unconvinced and stay involved.

How much work is it really?

Roughly one to two and a half hours of preparation a week — reading, a short quiz and an exercise — plus the 75-minute session. If that is not realistic right now, take a later cohort: arriving without having done the exercise is worse than not arriving.

What if I miss a week?

It happens — tell your facilitator. Missing more than a couple makes it hard for the group, so if you know it will be a bad six weeks, take the next cohort instead.

Is it only for students?

No. The fellowship is open to everyone, students and non-students alike, and cohorts usually have a mix of both.
